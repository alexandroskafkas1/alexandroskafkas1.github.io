Course Description & Student Learning Outcomes

Course Description: Single variable calculus especially for students of science, engineering, mathematics, and computer science. Integral calculus with applications. Satisfies Volunteer Core Requirement for QR. (RE) Prerequisite(s): MATH 132 or MATH 141 or MATH 147.

Prerequisites Details: Math 141, or Calculus I. You should be very familiar with different methods of differentiation and L'Hôpital's Rule. You should have memorized the Differentiation Rules found in the Formula Cards section of your ebook.

Student Learning Outcomes

By the end of this course, you should be able to:

  • Use the definition of definite integral as a Riemann sum and interpret graphically.
  • Classify and evaluate definite and indefinite integrals using the techniques of substitution, integration by parts, trigonometric identities and substitution, and partial fractions.
  • Approximate the value of a definite integral numerically using left- and right-sums, midpoint rule, and using technology.
  • Use integrals to solve a variety of application problems (areas, volumes, work, arc length, physics applications).
  • Analyze a sequence or series and apply the correct test to determine whether it converges or diverges. Find the limit of a sequence and the sum of a series when possible.
  • Find a Taylor series for a function and its interval of convergence, and use it to approximate values and evaluate integrals.

Value Proposition

You will master integral calculus as applied to polynomial, rational, radical, exponential, logarithmic and trigonometric functions and series. This includes computing integrals of these functions and determining whether series converge or diverge. You will develop analytical and critical thinking skills and broaden problem solving techniques as they apply calculus to applications of areas, volumes, and physics problems.
