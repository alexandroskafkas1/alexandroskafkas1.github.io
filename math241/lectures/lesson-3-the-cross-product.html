Lesson 3

The Cross Product & Triple Products

Goals

  • Evaluate \(2 \times 2\) and \(3 \times 3\) determinants as geometric scaling factors.
  • Define the cross product algebraically and verify its fundamental orthogonality property.
  • Relate cross product magnitude to parallelogram and triangle areas.
  • Compute scalar triple products and interpret them as oriented volumes.
  • Apply the cross product to rotational mechanics (torque).

Determinant Preliminaries

The cross product in \(\mathbb{R}^3\) is conveniently defined via formal matrix determinants. Recall the evaluation formulas:

  1. Order 2: \[ \det \begin{pmatrix} a & b \\ c & d \end{pmatrix} = ad - bc \]
  2. Order 3 (Cofactor Expansion along Row 1): \[ \det \begin{pmatrix} a_1 & a_2 & a_3 \\ b_1 & b_2 & b_3 \\ c_1 & c_2 & c_3 \end{pmatrix} = a_1 \begin{vmatrix} b_2 & b_3 \\ c_2 & c_3 \end{vmatrix} - a_2 \begin{vmatrix} b_1 & b_3 \\ c_1 & c_3 \end{vmatrix} + a_3 \begin{vmatrix} b_1 & b_2 \\ c_1 & c_2 \end{vmatrix} \]

Definition of the Cross Product

Unlike the dot product—which takes two vectors to a scalar and exists in every dimension—the cross product is an operation specific to three-dimensional Euclidean space \(\mathbb{R}^3\) that takes two vectors and outputs another vector.

Definition: Cross Product

Let \(\vec{a} = \langle a_1, a_2, a_3 \rangle\) and \(\vec{b} = \langle b_1, b_2, b_3 \rangle\) in \(\mathbb{R}^3\). The cross product \(\vec{a} \times \vec{b}\) is the formal determinant:

\[ \vec{a} \times \vec{b} \coloneq \begin{vmatrix} \hat{i} & \hat{j} & \hat{k} \\ a_1 & a_2 & a_3 \\ b_1 & b_2 & b_3 \end{vmatrix} = \langle a_2b_3 - a_3b_2,\; a_3b_1 - a_1b_3,\; a_1b_2 - a_2b_1 \rangle \]
Example 1

Compute \(\vec{u} \times \vec{v}\) for \(\vec{u} = \langle 2, -1, 3 \rangle\) and \(\vec{v} = \langle 4, 0, -2 \rangle\).

Solution \[ \begin{aligned} \vec{u} \times \vec{v} &= \begin{vmatrix} \hat{i} & \hat{j} & \hat{k} \\ 2 & -1 & 3 \\ 4 & 0 & -2 \end{vmatrix} \\ &= \hat{i}\begin{vmatrix} -1 & 3 \\ 0 & -2 \end{vmatrix} - \hat{j}\begin{vmatrix} 2 & 3 \\ 4 & -2 \end{vmatrix} + \hat{k}\begin{vmatrix} 2 & -1 \\ 4 & 0 \end{vmatrix} \\ &= \hat{i}(2 - 0) - \hat{j}(-4 - 12) + \hat{k}(0 - (-4)) \\ &= 2\hat{i} + 16\hat{j} + 4\hat{k} = \langle 2, 16, 4 \rangle \end{aligned} \]
Proposition: Nilpotency

For any vector \(\vec{a} \in \mathbb{R}^3\), \(\vec{a} \times \vec{a} = \mathbf{0}\).

Proof. By direct component substitution: \(a_2a_3 - a_3a_2 = 0\), \(a_3a_1 - a_1a_3 = 0\), and \(a_1a_2 - a_2a_1 = 0\). Thus \(\vec{a} \times \vec{a} = \langle 0, 0, 0 \rangle = \mathbf{0}\).

Geometric Properties: Direction and Magnitude

Theorem: Orthogonality of the Cross Product

The vector \(\vec{a} \times \vec{b}\) is orthogonal to both \(\vec{a}\) and \(\vec{b}\):

\[ \vec{a} \cdot (\vec{a} \times \vec{b}) = 0 \quad \text{and} \quad \vec{b} \cdot (\vec{a} \times \vec{b}) = 0 \]

Proof. Expanding the scalar product directly:

\[ \vec{a} \cdot (\vec{a} \times \vec{b}) = a_1(a_2b_3 - a_3b_2) + a_2(a_3b_1 - a_1b_3) + a_3(a_1b_2 - a_2b_1) \]

Multiplying out the terms produces exact pairs that cancel identically:

\[ a_1a_2b_3 - a_1a_3b_2 + a_2a_3b_1 - a_1a_2b_3 + a_1a_3b_2 - a_2a_3b_1 = 0 \]

An identical cancellation yields \(\vec{b} \cdot (\vec{a} \times \vec{b}) = 0\). The orientation of \(\vec{a} \times \vec{b}\) relative to \(\{\vec{a}, \vec{b}\}\) is fixed by the standard right-hand rule.

Theorem: Magnitude as Area

If \(\theta \in [0, \pi]\) is the angle between \(\vec{a}\) and \(\vec{b}\), then:

\[ \|\vec{a} \times \vec{b}\| = \|\vec{a}\|\|\vec{b}\|\sin\theta \]

Geometrically, \(\|\vec{b}\|\sin\theta\) is the altitude of the parallelogram spanned by displacements \(\vec{a}\) and \(\vec{b}\). Hence, \(\|\vec{a} \times \vec{b}\|\) equals the area of the spanned parallelogram.

Corollary: Collinearity Criterion

Two non-zero vectors \(\vec{a}\) and \(\vec{b}\) are parallel (collinear) if and only if:

\[ \vec{a} \times \vec{b} = \mathbf{0} \]
Example 2

Find the area of the triangle with vertices \(A=(2,-1,0)\), \(B=(9,3,0)\), and \(C=(5,11,0)\).

Solution

Form the displacement vectors along two edges:

\[ \overrightarrow{AB} = \langle 9-2,\ 3-(-1),\ 0-0 \rangle = \langle 7, 4, 0 \rangle \] \[ \overrightarrow{AC} = \langle 5-2,\ 11-(-1),\ 0-0 \rangle = \langle 3, 12, 0 \rangle \]

The cross product spans the parallelogram:

\[ \overrightarrow{AB} \times \overrightarrow{AC} = \begin{vmatrix} \hat{i} & \hat{j} & \hat{k} \\ 7 & 4 & 0 \\ 3 & 12 & 0 \end{vmatrix} = \hat{k}\,(7\cdot 12 - 4\cdot 3) = 72\hat{k} \]

The triangle has half the area of this parallelogram:

\[ \text{Area} = \frac{1}{2}\|\overrightarrow{AB} \times \overrightarrow{AC}\| = \frac{1}{2}(72) = 36 \]

Algebraic Laws of the Cross Product

Let \(\vec{a}, \vec{b}, \vec{c} \in \mathbb{R}^3\) and \(\lambda \in \mathbb{R}\):

  1. Anticommutativity: \(\vec{a} \times \vec{b} = -(\vec{b} \times \vec{a})\)
  2. Compatibility with Scaling: \((\lambda\vec{a}) \times \vec{b} = \lambda(\vec{a} \times \vec{b}) = \vec{a} \times (\lambda\vec{b})\)
  3. Distributivity: \(\vec{a} \times (\vec{b} + \vec{c}) = \vec{a} \times \vec{b} + \vec{a} \times \vec{c}\) and \((\vec{a} + \vec{b}) \times \vec{c} = \vec{a} \times \vec{c} + \vec{b} \times \vec{c}\)
  4. Cyclic Invariance of Triple Product: \(\vec{a} \cdot (\vec{b} \times \vec{c}) = (\vec{a} \times \vec{b}) \cdot \vec{c}\)

The Scalar Triple Product: Volume

Definition: Scalar Triple Product

The scalar triple product of three vectors \(\vec{a}, \vec{b}, \vec{c} \in \mathbb{R}^3\) is the determinant of the matrix formed by their components:

\[ \vec{a} \cdot (\vec{b} \times \vec{c}) = \begin{vmatrix} a_1 & a_2 & a_3 \\ b_1 & b_2 & b_3 \\ c_1 & c_2 & c_3 \end{vmatrix} \]

The absolute value \(|\vec{a} \cdot (\vec{b} \times \vec{c})|\) measures the volume of the parallelepiped spanned by \(\vec{a}\), \(\vec{b}\), and \(\vec{c}\). If the scalar triple product is zero, the three vectors are coplanar.

Example 3

Find the volume of the parallelepiped determined by \(\vec{a} = \langle 2, 1, 0 \rangle\), \(\vec{b} = \langle 1, 3, 1 \rangle\), and \(\vec{c} = \langle 0, 2, 4 \rangle\).

Solution

Evaluate the determinant directly:

\[ \begin{aligned} \vec{a} \cdot (\vec{b} \times \vec{c}) &= \begin{vmatrix} 2 & 1 & 0 \\ 1 & 3 & 1 \\ 0 & 2 & 4 \end{vmatrix} \\ &= 2\begin{vmatrix} 3 & 1 \\ 2 & 4 \end{vmatrix} - 1\begin{vmatrix} 1 & 1 \\ 0 & 4 \end{vmatrix} + 0 \\ &= 2(12 - 2) - 1(4 - 0) = 20 - 4 = 16 \end{aligned} \]

Therefore, the volume is \(V = |16| = 16\).

Application in Mechanics: Torque

When a force \(\vec{F}\) acts at a point located at position \(\vec{r}\) relative to a reference pivot, the rotational tendency is quantified by the torque vector:

\[ \boldsymbol{\tau} = \vec{r} \times \vec{F} \]

The magnitude \(\|\boldsymbol{\tau}\| = \|\vec{r}\|\|\vec{F}\|\sin\theta\) accounts for the moment arm, while the direction indicates the axis of rotation dictated by the right-hand rule.

Example 4

A force \(\vec{F} = \langle 0, 5, 0 \rangle\text{ N}\) is applied at position \(\vec{r} = \langle 3, 0, 0 \rangle\text{ m}\) relative to a pivot at the origin. Compute the resulting torque \(\boldsymbol{\tau}\).

Solution \[ \boldsymbol{\tau} = \vec{r} \times \vec{F} = \begin{vmatrix} \hat{i} & \hat{j} & \hat{k} \\ 3 & 0 & 0 \\ 0 & 5 & 0 \end{vmatrix} = \hat{k}(15 - 0) = \langle 0, 0, 15 \rangle\text{ N}\cdot\text{m} \]

The torque has a magnitude of \(15\text{ N}\cdot\text{m}\) directed along the positive \(z\)-axis (counterclockwise rotation in the \(xy\)-plane).
