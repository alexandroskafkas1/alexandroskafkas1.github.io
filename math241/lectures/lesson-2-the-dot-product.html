Definition: Dot Product

For vectors \(\vec{u} = \langle u_1, u_2, \dots, u_n \rangle\) and \(\vec{w} = \langle w_1, w_2, \dots, w_n \rangle\) in \(\mathbb{R}^n\), their dot product is the [...] \[ \vec{u} \cdot \vec{w} := \sum_{i=1}^n u_i w_i = u_1w_1 + u_2w_2 + \dots + u_nw_n \]

Example 1

Compute the dot product for each pair of vectors:

  1. \(\vec{u} = \langle 2, 5 \rangle\) and \(\vec{w} = \langle -2, 4 \rangle\)
  2. \(\vec{a} = 4\hat{i} - \hat{j} + 3\hat{k}\) and \(\vec{b} = 2\hat{i} + 3\hat{j} - \hat{k}\)
Solution
  1. \(\vec{u} \cdot \vec{w} = (2)(-2) + (5)(4) = -4 + 20 = 16\)
  2. \(\vec{a} \cdot \vec{b} = (4)(2) + (-1)(3) + (3)(-1) = 8 - 3 - 3 = 2\)

Algebraic Laws

Directly from the field axioms of \(\mathbb{R}\), the dot product satisfies the following identities for all \(\vec{u}, \vec{v}, \vec{w} \in \mathbb{R}^n\) and \(\lambda \in \mathbb{R}[...]

  1. Symmetry (Commutativity): \(\vec{u} \cdot \vec{w} = \vec{w} \cdot \vec{u}\)
  2. Bilinearity (Distributivity): \(\vec{u} \cdot (\vec{v} + \vec{w}) = \vec{u} \cdot \vec{v} + \vec{u} \cdot \vec{w}\)
  3. Homogeneity: \((\lambda\vec{u}) \cdot \vec{w} = \lambda(\vec{u} \cdot \vec{w})\)
  4. Positive Definiteness: \(\vec{u} \cdot \vec{u} = \|\vec{u}\|^2 \ge 0\), with equality if and only if \(\vec{u} = \mathbf{0}\).

Theorem: Geometric Dot Product

If \(\theta \in [0, \pi]\) is the angle between non-zero vectors \(\vec{u}\) and \(\vec{w}\), then:

\[ \vec{u} \cdot \vec{w} = \|\vec{u}\|\|\vec{w}\|\cos\theta \]

Solving for \(\cos\theta\) allows us to determine the angle between any two directional displacements without constructing physical triangles:

\[ \cos\theta = \frac{\vec{u} \cdot \vec{w}}{\|\vec{u}\|\|\vec{w}\|} = \left(\frac{\vec{u}}{\|\vec{u}\|}\right) \cdot \left(\frac{\vec{w}}{\|\vec{w}\|}\right) \]

That is, \(\cos\theta\) is simply the dot product of the respective unit direction vectors.

Example 2

Find the angle between \(\vec{a} = \langle 1, 1, 0 \rangle\) and \(\vec{b} = \langle 0, 1, 1 \rangle\).

Solution

Compute the components:

\[ \vec{a} \cdot \vec{b} = (1)(0) + (1)(1) + (0)(1) = 1 \] \[ \|\vec{a}\| = \sqrt{1^2 + 1^2 + 0^2} = \sqrt{2}, \quad \|\vec{b}\| = \sqrt{0^2 + 1^2 + 1^2} = \sqrt{2} \] \[ \cos\theta = \frac{\vec{a} \cdot \vec{b}}{\|\vec{a}\|\|\vec{b}\|} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}\sqrt{2}} = \frac{1}{2} \]

Since \(\cos\theta = \frac{1}{2}\) and \(\theta \in [0, \pi]\), we find \(\theta = \frac{\pi}{3} = 60^\circ\).

Definition: Orthogonality

Two vectors \(\vec{u}\) and \(\vec{w}\) are orthogonal (perpendicular, written \(\vec{u} \perp \vec{w}\)) if and only if:

\[ \vec{u} \cdot \vec{w} = 0 \]

Example 3

Determine whether each pair of vectors is orthogonal:

  1. \(\vec{u} = \langle 1, 2, 3 \rangle\) and \(\vec{v} = \langle 4, -5, 2 \rangle\)
  2. \(\vec{u} = 2\hat{i} - 5\hat{j}\) and \(\vec{v} = 5\hat{i} + 2\hat{j}\)
Solution
  1. \(\vec{u} \cdot \vec{v} = (1)(4) + (2)(-5) + (3)(2) = 4 - 10 + 6 = 0\).
    Because their dot product is zero, \(\vec{u} \perp \vec{v}\).
  2. \(\vec{u} \cdot \vec{v} = (2)(5) + (-5)(2) = 10 - 10 = 0\).
    The vectors are orthogonal.
